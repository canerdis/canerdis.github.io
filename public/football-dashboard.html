What pooling hides

Finishing skill looks weakly related to scoring — until you stop analysing goalkeepers, defenders and strikers as one undifferentiated pool. Segmenting by position and controlling for playing time roughly doubles the measured effect. The lesson is about aggregation, not about football.

Four steps from 0.303 to 0.618

Each row narrows the population by one decision. Nothing is re-modelled — the same two columns are correlated throughout.

Correlation is descriptive. Finishing ratings are assigned by observers who watched these players score, so this measures agreement between a scout's judgement and an outcome — not a causal effect.

Why the pooled number is wrong

The four positions barely overlap on the finishing scale, and each converts it to goals at a different rate. Averaging across them measures the mix, not the relationship.

Players with at least 10 starts; buckets with fewer than 30 player-seasons are dropped. Goalkeepers sit flat on zero across their whole range — they are 9% of the data and contribute nothing but noise to a pooled estimate.

Every position rewards its own attributes

Correlation of each attribute with goals scored, computed inside each position. That the diagonal blocks light up is the validity check: a classifier that was mislabelling players would not produce this.

Tackling and marking track goals for defenders and work against them for midfielders — defenders who win the ball are simply the better players on the pitch, and the ones who score set-pieces.

Possession is a weak predictor

Bucket averages rise cleanly with the share of the ball. The match-level correlation is only 0.26 — the tidy staircase is an artefact of averaging thousands of noisy matches.

This chart is its own warning. Binned means almost always look more decisive than the underlying relationship — the same flattery that makes the pooled 0.303 look like a finding.

Method. Goal events are parsed from the Match.goal XML and filtered on goal_type: n and p are credited to the scorer, o (own goals) are excluded from their tally, and npm / dg (missed penalties, disallowed goals) are not goals at all. Keeping n + p + o reproduces the recorded scoreline on 99.9% of matches against 91.6% for a naive parse — that reconciliation is what identified the excluded types. Appearances are starting-XI only; this schema has no substitute records.
