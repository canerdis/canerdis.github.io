Twenty years ago Finland led Europe in all three PISA subjects and Türkiye sat near the bottom of each. In 2025 they are 10 points apart in science and 7 in reading. Across 32 European systems and three subjects, 82 of 96 scores are lower than in 2006. A gap of 20 points is roughly one year of schooling.

| Change since 2006 | Science | Mathematics | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| OECD average | -11 | -30 | -25 |
| Finland | -59 | -73 | -80 |
| Türkiye | +70 | +38 | +25 |
| Leader in 2025 | Estonia | Estonia | Ireland |

## Steepest at the top

Every one of Europe's 2006 top ten fell in every subject; the Nordic five lost 32 points in science, 51 in reading and 48 in mathematics. In 2006, 33 country-subject scores in Europe were above 500; in 2025, six are. Only Estonia and the United Kingdom held their ground, and only in science and reading.

## Less a decline than a compression

Of the 14 scores that rose, 13 belong to countries that started in the bottom eight. The gap between Europe's best and worst system shrank by about 45 points in each subject.

## Finland and Türkiye

Finland's fall is the largest on all three charts and runs through every cycle since 2006. It is steepest among weaker students: the bottom tenth lost between 89 and 104 points, the top tenth between 35 and 63, and boys fell furthest. Finnish researchers point to collapsing reading habits, falling motivation and an inclusion reform introduced as budgets were cut.

Türkiye rose in all three subjects, and did so while PISA's coverage of Turkish 15-year-olds widened from 47% to 72%, which normally pulls a score down. Twelve years of compulsory schooling, wider preschool and an exam system that has drilled PISA-style questions since 2018 all played a part.

## Caveats

The pandemic falls inside this window, but it does not explain the slide on its own: Finland fell in every cycle since 2006, long before schools closed. The Netherlands and Norway did not fully meet PISA's sampling standards in 2025. The OECD average covers 35 members in science and mathematics but 23 in reading, so it is not the same group in every subject.
