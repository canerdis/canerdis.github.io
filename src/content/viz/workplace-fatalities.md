Around 3 million people die from workplace accidents worldwide every year. In the
European Union the figure was 3,298 in 2023. EU labour regulation is meant to
standardise safety across member states, so the interesting question is how much
variation survives that standardisation.

A good deal, as it turns out. Within the EU, France reports the highest rate at
**3.6** deaths per 100,000 workers, followed by Bulgaria (3.51) and Cyprus (3.24).

Outside the bloc the gap widens sharply. **Türkiye records 11.47** — more than three
times the highest EU rate — with Ukraine at 7.1 and Russia at 5.03.

The industries behind the numbers are consistent everywhere: construction,
transport and storage, and manufacturing account for most fatal accidents.

One note on reading the map: the United Kingdom is shown in grey rather than
shaded. Its most recent available data is from 2018 and is not comparable with the
other countries, so colouring it would have implied a comparison the data does not
support.
