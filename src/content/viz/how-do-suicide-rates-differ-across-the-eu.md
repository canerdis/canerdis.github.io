Around 49,000 people in the EU died by suicide in 2023, three quarters of them men. The charts show the rate in each country as deaths per 100,000 men and per 100,000 women, adjusted for the age of each country's population so that countries with older and younger populations can be compared fairly.

| | Highest rate | EU average |
|---|---|---|
| Men | Lithuania, 33.6 | 16.9 |
| Women | Iceland, 9.7 | 4.7 |

## Where rates are highest

For men, the highest rates are in the Baltics and central Europe. For women, they are in the Nordic countries. The two patterns barely overlap, and shown as a single total they would blur into each other.

## The gap between men and women

The gap varies widely. It is largest in Malta, Moldova and Latvia, where men die by suicide seven to eight times as often as women. In Iceland it is closer to twice as often, and in the Netherlands closer to two and a half times.

## Reading the charts

The rates are age-standardised: they compare countries as if all had the same age structure, so they are not raw counts. The two charts use different scales, 0 to 35 for men and 0 to 10 for women, so bar lengths cannot be compared between them. Recording practices also differ between countries, which can affect how some deaths are classified.
