I applied to 463 internships this year and got through the first stage of 24 of
them. The result was zero.

Being a statistics student, I logged my own process as it happened, then put the
log through a Sankey diagram. This is what came out.

The most striking part is at the very beginning. **439 of the 463 applications
were rejected outright or never answered at all** — no stage, no test, no reply.
Only about **5%** of first applications produce any response, and most of the
time the process does not end with a clear "no". It ends with silence.

The 24 that did respond then dissolved one at a time: aptitude and English tests,
AI and video interviews, a department interview, a case study. At each stage the
same small hope, then the same result.

| Stage | Remaining |
|---|---|
| Applications sent | 463 |
| Aptitude & English test | 24 |
| AI & video interview | 14 |
| Department interview | 3 |
| Case study | 2 |
| Offers | **0** |

There is no success story here. This is the current state of the market, recorded
from a student's side of it.

The chart is in Turkish, as it was published. The Sankey form is doing the work a
funnel chart usually gets asked to do, but a funnel would hide where people leave;
the flow makes the drop-off at the very first step impossible to miss.

Posted on LinkedIn, where it drew **832 reactions and 22 comments**, and around
125,000 impressions.

Hard as it is to get used to, I am still applying.
