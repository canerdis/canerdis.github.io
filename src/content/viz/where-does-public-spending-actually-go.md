Governments publish their budgets and almost nobody reads them. This chart turns 2024 public spending in 30 European countries into shares of the total: what every hundred euros actually buys.

## The largest slice is the same everywhere

Social protection, which covers pensions, unemployment, family and sickness benefits, is the biggest single item in all thirty countries, from 45.8% of spending in Finland down to 26.0% in Hungary.

## Second place is where countries split

| Second-largest item | Countries | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Health | 20 | Ireland spends the most on it, at 24.3% |
| Economic affairs | 6 | Led by Malta, at 20.3% |
| Running the state and servicing its debt | 3 | |
| Education | 1 | Switzerland, where health care runs largely through mandatory private insurance rather than the government's books |

## Debt interest is the one to watch

Hungary now spends 10.5% of its budget on interest on old debt, more than it spends on education: the only country on the chart where that is true. Iceland follows at 10.1%, Italy at 7.9% and Greece at 7.5%.

## Reading the chart

In a stacked bar only the first segment starts from the same point, so debt interest, on the far left, is the easiest category to compare between countries; for the rest, the figures above do the comparing. The chart shows shares, not amounts: a large share of a small budget can still be less money per person than a smaller share of a large one. "Other" covers public order, environment, housing and culture.
