Median disposable income tells you the level. It does not tell you how that income
is spread, and two countries with the same median can look very different once you
ask who holds it.

The Gini index answers that. A coefficient of 0 describes perfect equality, where
every income is identical. A coefficient of 1 describes the opposite extreme, where
a single person holds everything and everyone else holds nothing. Real countries sit
between those poles, and the spread across Europe is wide enough to be worth mapping.

This map shows the most recent available year for each country, which is a caveat
worth stating plainly: the years are not identical everywhere, so small differences
between neighbours are not always comparable. The broad pattern is what the map is
for, not the ranking of any two countries a fraction apart.
