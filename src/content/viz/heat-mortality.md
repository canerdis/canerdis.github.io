Roughly **13,589** people die of heat-related causes each year across 854 European
cities. That total is the headline, but it hides the more useful finding, which is
how unevenly it lands.

Age-standardised heat-related mortality runs from **28.4 per 100,000 in Croatia**
down to **0.8 in Ireland** — a thirty-five-fold difference across the same
continent. The gradient runs southeast to northwest, which tracks climate, but also
building stock, urban density and how much of the population is elderly.

That last factor dominates. People aged 75 and over account for roughly
**two-thirds** of heat-related deaths, which means the map is partly a map of where
Europe's older population lives, not only of where it gets hottest.

Figures are age-standardised over 2000–2020, so they describe a two-decade average
rather than any single heatwave.
