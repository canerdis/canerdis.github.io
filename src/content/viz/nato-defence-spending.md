Ranking NATO's 32 members by core defence spending as a share of GDP produces a
league table that maps almost perfectly onto geography.

**Lithuania leads at 5.33%**, followed by Estonia (5.10), Latvia (4.92) and
Poland (4.68) — all on the eastern flank. **Greece** is the fifth above the line
at 3.65, and the only one of the five that does not border the Baltic or Russia.

Those five are the whole list. Denmark (3.49), Sweden (3.22), Norway and the
United States (both 3.17) sit just under the **3.5%** benchmark, while the
largest western European economies fall well below it: **France 2.22, Italy 2.10,
Spain 2.00**.

Two caveats matter for reading the chart. These are 2026 estimates in constant 2021
prices, so they are projections rather than settled accounts. And 3.5% is only part
of the picture: NATO's wider 5% goal by 2035 combines core defence spending with up
to a further 1.5% of broader security expenditure, which is measured differently and
is not shown here.
